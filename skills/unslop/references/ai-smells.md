# AI Smells — Patterns to Remove

These are common AI tells. Remove or rewrite them so the underlying technical point comes through in natural, connected prose.

## Vocabulary Clusters
delve, tapestry, landscape (metaphorical), pivotal, crucial, underscore, vibrant, meticulous, intricate, testament, garner, bolster, foster, showcase, highlight, emphasize, enhance, enduring, nuanced, multifaceted, comprehensive, robust, leverage, realm, paradigm, cornerstone, beacon, spearhead, demystify, unpack, navigate (metaphorical), harness (as a verb: "harness the power of"; the noun "AI harness" is a real technical term), catalyze, synergy, holistic, granular, deep dive, unravel, groundbreaking, revolutionary, transformative, game-changing, unprecedented, remarkable, extraordinary, pioneering, furthermore, moreover, it's worth noting.

## Theatrical Metaphors & Blog Clichés (Never Substitute These for Buzzwords)
- Dramatic idioms ("what the hand gives, the hand takes away", "double-edged sword", "silver bullet", "drinking from the firehose").
- Fabricated war-room clichés ("the difference between a build that ships and one that stalls at 3 PM", "spins like a centrifuge", "battle-tested in the trenches").
- Over-eager blog hooks inside technical dossiers or architecture readouts.

## Structural Patterns
- **Staccato STE chopping (the over-correction smell)**: Chopping a connected cause-and-effect thought into three 8-word robotic sentences with hard periods instead of linking them naturally with `"because"`, `"whereas"`, `"so that"`, or `"which means"`.
- Throat-clearing openings ("In today's fast-paced...", "In an increasingly...").
- Rule-of-three lists used as filler ("innovative, transformative, and groundbreaking").
- "It's not just X — it's Y" constructions.
- Vague attribution ("studies show", "experts agree", "it is widely known").
- Significance inflation ("stands as a testament", "plays a vital role", "underscores the importance").
- Restating summaries that add no new information ("In summary", "Overall", "In conclusion" that just repeats).
- Superficial `-ing` participial tails ("...highlighting the importance of...", "...ensuring that...").
- Typographic em-dashes (`—`) used for dramatic pauses where a spaced hyphen (` - `), comma, colon, or parenthesis works better.
- Hedging stacks ("might potentially", "could possibly", "it remains to be seen" on settled points).

## Formatting Tells
- Markdown debris or tracking parameters left in text.
- Inline-header bullet lists that merely repeat the paragraph above.
- "Conclusion" sections that only restate the opening paragraph.

## Replacement Rule
Do not replace corporate buzzwords with theatrical metaphors or clipped 8-word fragments. State the mechanism and its practical consequence in one connected, concrete sentence. If a sentence adds no information, delete it.
