# AI Smells — Patterns to Remove

These are common tells. Remove or rewrite them. One isolated instance is often fine; clusters are not.

## Vocabulary Clusters
delve, tapestry, landscape (metaphorical), pivotal, crucial, underscore, vibrant, meticulous, intricate, testament, garner, bolster, foster, showcase, highlight, emphasize, enhance, enduring, nuanced, multifaceted, comprehensive, robust, leverage, realm, paradigm, cornerstone, beacon, spearhead, demystify, unpack, navigate (metaphorical), harness, catalyze, synergy, holistic, granular, deep dive, unravel, groundbreaking, revolutionary, transformative, game-changing, unprecedented, remarkable, extraordinary.

## Structural Patterns
- Throat-clearing openings ("In today's fast-paced...", "In an increasingly...").
- Rule-of-three lists used as filler ("innovative, transformative, and groundbreaking").
- "It's not just X — it's Y" constructions.
- Vague attribution ("studies show", "experts agree", "it is widely known").
- Significance inflation ("stands as a testament", "plays a vital role", "underscores the importance").
- Restating summaries that add no new information ("In summary", "Overall", "In conclusion" that just repeats).
- Superficial -ing analyses ("highlighting the importance of...", "ensuring that...").
- Overuse of em dashes for drama where a comma or period works better.
- Bolding the first few words of every bullet as a default.
- Hedging stacks ("might potentially", "could possibly", "it remains to be seen" on settled points).
- Promotional or breathless tone on neutral topics.

## Formatting Tells
- Markdown debris or tracking parameters left in text.
- Title Case Headings used excessively.
- Inline-header bullet lists that restate the paragraph above.
- "Conclusion" sections that only restate.

## Replacement Rule
Do not invent new tics. The cure for a cliché is usually its absence, not a substitute phrase. If a sentence can be deleted without loss of meaning, delete it.
